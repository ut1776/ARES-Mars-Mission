THE PROBLEM

A crew walks. Global maps don’t.

Today’s Mars portals show the whole planet. A suited explorer needs a few kilometres of ground described at the scale of a footstep: where it is walkable, where it is not, what is worth the walk, and what it will look like on arrival.

ONE VIEW, REAL NASA DATA

Five NASA sources. One decision.

OPTICAL

HiRISE image

Metre-scale picture of the ground. The map you see in the replay.

TOPOGRAPHIC

HiRISE DTM + MOLA

Slope and hazard at astronaut scale, with regional elevation context.

SPECTRAL

CRISM

Quality-masked mineral indices pick the science stops: olivine, hydrated silicate, carbonate.

GROUND TRUTH

Perseverance

Where the rover really drove, and what it saw. Used to test our hazard model.

ENVIRONMENT

MEDA weather · radiation

Temperature and pressure context, plus a published surface radiation rate for the dose clock.

SCIENTIFIC INTEGRITY

What is measured, what is not.

MEASURED

  • HiRISE elevation and imagery
  • CRISM summary parameters and quality mask
  • MOLA elevation
  • Perseverance waypoints and images

DERIVED

  • Slope, hazard fraction, no-go zones
  • Science-stop selection
  • Least-cost route, EVA clock

ASSUMED

    ARES is a planning prototype, not a certified safe route. Suit mass, Mars gravity, rock-scale hazards below the elevation-model resolution, thermal loads and live space weather are not modelled. CRISM indices say where to look, not what is there.

    WHAT COMES NEXT

    From planning table to helmet visor.

    The route, no-go mask and budget are plain rasters and JSON, so the same plan can drive a habitat display, a crew tablet or a visor HUD. Next: live radiation, thermal maps, rock-abundance hazards, a rover-assisted two-person EVA, and any other site, by changing the product IDs.
